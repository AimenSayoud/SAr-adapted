§ 0 · Overview

What this document is

The second-round critique made one structural charge and several specific ones. The structural charge was that our twelve tests had been graded by their authors, and that the design on which the headline result rests — matching a target zone against a land-cover-matched reference — had been asserted rather than tested.

Phase L was built to answer that charge first and everything else second: L1 is a gate, and the remaining tests were only to be read if the gate passed. It passed.

Every number below is produced by code in src/insar_wetlands/referee.py, exercised by tests/test_referee.py, and reproduced by notebooks/phaseL_gate.ipynb.

§ 1 · L1 · Answers R1 M5

The gate

Verdict: Gate Passed
The Objection

The seasonal amplitude is a difference between the mat and a reference zone. If the reference is not genuinely comparable, the difference measures the mismatch, not the mat. Round 1 asserted the match; it never tested whether a differently chosen matched reference would give the same answer.

The Test Protocol

Build 25 independent control patches from the land-cover-matched pool — same WorldCover class, same flatness and water screens, outside the mat, disjoint from the published reference C — and recompute the seasonal amplitude against each.

Visualized: Amplitude Stability (Matched vs Unmatched Control Pool) 25 Controls Clearing Null p95
Amplitude stability across independently chosen references
Quantity Phase K (unmatched) Phase L (matched)
Median amplitude — 3.115 mm
Spread range 2.52–10.16, sd 2.92 IQR 0.482
Controls clearing null p95 (2.025 mm) — 25 / 25
Empirical p — 0.0033
Verdict — Gate Passed
The amplitude is stable across independently chosen matched references: the spread collapses from sd 2.92 mm to an IQR of 0.48 mm once the control pool is matched on land cover. The design is now tested rather than asserted, which was the critique's central demand.

A diagnostic worth keeping. The matched null (0.855 mm) agrees closely with the published D-based null (0.850 mm), but a null built against the real zone C gives 1.533 mm. The extra variance is therefore a property of C's fragmentation, not of the null construction — a real limitation of the published reference, and it should be stated as one.

§ 2 · L2 · Border Contamination

Erode the mat

Answered
The Objection

Zone A's border pixels contain surrounding terrain through the resolution cell and sidelobes. If the coherence deficit and the seasonal amplitude are contamination artefacts, both should move when the border is stripped.

Visualized: Coherence Deficit Stability & Closure Dispersion under 0px, 1px, 2px Erosion Consistent with Resampling Noise
Successive one-pixel erosions
Quantity 0 px 1 px 2 px Reading
Coherence deficit −0.0809 −0.0832 −0.0789 stable, non-monotonic, 5 % swing
Closure dispersion (× ref.) 3.22 3.35 3.58 rises monotonically
Seasonal amplitude ×1.00 — ×1.15 noise alone predicts ×1.46
Verdict — Answered
The coherence deficit is not a border artefact: it does not weaken under erosion, and swings non-monotonically by 5 %, consistent with resampling noise rather than a contamination gradient. The closure dispersion rises monotonically — the opposite of what contamination predicts. The amplitude rises ×1.15 where pure noise predicts ×1.46, so the signal is partly, but not wholly, explained by the shrinking sample.
§ 3 · L3 · Makes M8 Conclusive

Subdivide the reference

Conclusive
The Objection

Round 1 subdivided only the target. A flat amplitude under target subdivision is consistent with a coherent signal — but also with a reference-side artefact that subdivision never touched.

Verdict — Conclusive
The amplitude is flat under subdivision of either side. A noise-floor artefact must rise as either patch shrinks; it does not. This closes M8 completely and is the cleanest positive result in Phase L.
§ 4 · L4 · Answers R1 S7

Closed triplets

Answered (Inverted)
The Objection

The closure-phase test found no significant bias. If the network contained few closed triplets, that null result would be uninformative — an absence of power, not an absence of bias.

Visualized: Closed Triplet Count (Observed vs Equivalent Random Network) 6.28× Enrichment

Result: 518 closed triplets, verified two independent ways — trace(A³)/6 and direct enumeration — which agree exactly. That is 6.28× more than a random network of the same node and edge count.

Verdict — Answered
The network is unusually well closed, not poorly closed. The closure null result is not explained by a shortage of triplets. Note this inverts the direction the objection assumed.
This does not yet settle §4.8. Whether the closure result is discriminating and negative or underpowered for the effect size in question depends on the moisture-phase literature (De Zan 2014; Zwieback 2015, 2017), which we have not yet worked through.
§ 5 · L5 · Closes M4 Hole

Amplitude without the network inversion

Inconclusive
The Objection

The published amplitude comes from the network inversion, so our "wrapped phase is immune to unwrapping errors" defence does not cover it.

Seasonal amplitude, two estimators
Estimator Amplitude
Wrapped, no inversion 2.32 mm
Network inversion (published) 3.29 mm

The difference is 1.94 σ of the wrapped estimator, whose fit quality is r² = 0.0053.

Verdict — Inconclusive (Reported Honestly)
The wrapped estimator is far too noisy to either confirm or refute the network value — an r² of 0.005 means it explains essentially none of the variance. 1.94 σ is not a contradiction, but neither is it a corroboration. The M4 hole is narrowed, not closed.

§ 7 · L7 · Answers R1 M6

The 27 pixels

Open (Fair Null Pending)

This test went through four self-corrections. Each is recorded because the sequence is the most instructive part of Phase L.

Correction 01 Invalid Test
The original test was invalid

Mann–Whitney on 27 pixels, p = 0.004. The pixels were selected by a threshold on temporal coherence and scored against a spatially autocorrelated distance field.

Correction 02 Directional Inversion
A two-sided statistic answered a directional question

The toroidal permutation returned p = 0.262, which we first read as "the concentration does not survive". That reading was wrong. Signed distance is negative inside the zone, so closer to the margin means a higher value — and the observed −40.0 m sits above the null p95 of −56.6 m. An observation beyond the null's 95th percentile cannot have p = 0.262 for a directional question.

The statistic was two-sided on |deviation from the null median|, and the null is strongly left-skewed (|p05 − median| = 166.7 m against |observed − median| = 49.4 m), so the long inward tail — draws deviating in the opposite direction to the hypothesis — inflated the count.

Correction 03 Floor Bounding
The one-sided p-value is censored, not estimated

p_greater = 1/4001 exactly: zero of 4 000 draws reached −40 m. The permutation therefore bounds p from above and cannot estimate it. Correct statement: p < 2.5 × 10⁻⁴ (0/4000) — never "p = 0.00025".

Correction 04 Shape Mismatch
The null could not match the observation's shape

The null had fallen back to compact blobs, because no rigid shift of the observed set fits inside zone A. Shape descriptors show why that control is invalid.

Observed set against the compact-blob null
Descriptor Observed Compact null
Pixels 27 27
Connected components 15 1
Radius of gyration 11.81 2.15 (×5.50)
Fill fraction 0.029 0.643
Bounding box 25 × 37 px —

A set of 15 scattered fragments can reach parts of a distance field that a single 27-pixel blob cannot occupy at any position. The p-value measured shape as much as position — which is exactly how it came to be pinned at the floor.

We Conceded Too Much in Round 1
M6 objected that 27 clustered pixels at a 160 m correlation length are not 27 independent observations. That premise is wrong. The 27 pixels are 15 separate fragments — mostly isolated pixels and pairs — spread over a 1000 × 1480 m box. Most fragments are separated by more than the correlation length, so the effective sample size is nearer 15 than 1: the Mann–Whitney was inflated by roughly a factor of two, not by a factor of 27. It was still not a valid test, but a 27-fold concession was an overcorrection on our part.
Status — Open, Pending Re-Run
A component-matched null is now implemented: it reproduces the observed fragment count and fragment sizes at random disjoint positions, preserving what fixes the effective sample size while randomising position. It has not yet run on the real data — the last Colab execution predates it. Until it does, the honest statement is that the Mann–Whitney was invalid though less so than conceded; the marginal concentration is in the predicted direction and is large (−40 m against a null median of −89 m); but no null matched on both size and shape has yet been constructed, so it stands as an observation, not a significant permutation result.

A limitation no statistic can remove: The observed median distance is 40 m — exactly one pixel. An anchored margin that holds phase, and border pixels contaminated by stable surrounding terrain, predict the same thing at this resolution. L7 cannot separate them whatever p-value it returns.

§ 8 · L8 · Answers R1 S4

Three intervals, not one

Answered (Choice Unstated)
The Objection

An i.i.d. bootstrap over dates destroys the temporal sampling structure and will understate the interval.

Visualized: 95% Interval Width Comparison (Analytic vs Bootstrap) Bootstrap is 3.2× Wider
95 % intervals on the seasonal amplitude
Method Interval (LOS, mm) Width
Analytic [2.22, 4.35] 2.13
Bootstrap [0.58, 7.32] 6.74 (×3.2)

Propagating the analytic upper limit: 4.35 mm LOS → 5.14 mm vertical (incidence 32.3°, factor 1.183) → 10.3 mm at an assumed coupling fraction f = 0.5.

Decision Required Before Submission
Which interval the manuscript quotes must be stated explicitly and justified. Quoting the analytic interval while the bootstrap is 3.2× wider needs a reason in the text; the conservative choice for an upper bound is the wider one. This is an authors' call, not a computational result, and it is currently unstated.
§ 9 · Editorial Cleanliness

Claims withdrawn from the manuscript

All instances removed and verified absent from docs/paper/:

  • "one to two orders of magnitude too small for flotation"
  • the "≤ 3.9 mm" and "< 2.4 mm" bounds
  • "A ≈ noise, C ≈ signal"
  • "B sits at the floor"
  • "below 4 mm"

§ 12 · Integrity

Methodological note for the referees

Four of the corrections in Phase L were to our own tests, found by our own diagnostics, after those tests had already produced a publishable-looking number:

  • A two-sided statistic answering a directional question on a skewed null — it reversed the verdict.
  • A p-value pinned at the permutation floor, reported as if estimated.
  • A null that did not match the observation on the property doing the work.
  • A first version of the fairness check that used whole-set spread — which for a fragmented set is a function of position, the hypothesis under test, and would have suppressed a true result. Caught by a synthetic case before it touched the data.

We report these rather than quietly fixing them, for the same reason Appendix A.13 records the errors corrected during the original analysis: a protocol that never surprises its authors is not being tested.
